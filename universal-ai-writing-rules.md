# Universal AI Writing Rules

These rules help avoid obvious AI patterns in all forms of writing — social copy, articles, essays, and more.

## Formatting

- No em dashes. Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead.
- No emojis except in social media captions (use sparingly; 2-3).
- Don't mechanically bold key terms. Use bold sparingly or not at all.

## Structural Patterns to Avoid

### No rule-of-three
Don't group things into trios ("efficient, effective, and reliable"). Two is fine. Four is fine. Three every time is a giveaway.

### No contrast framing
Don't write "It's not about X, it's about Y." Don't write "This isn't X, it's Y." Don't write the escalation version: "It's not A. It's not even B. It's actually C." Just say what you mean.

All variants are banned: "Not just X, but Y," "Not only X, but also Y," "More than just X, it's Y."

### No staccato bursts
Don't string together three or more short sentences for dramatic effect. Vary sentence length naturally.

### No fake naming
Don't give ideas a capitalized name with "The" in front of it. "The Growth Paradox," "The 5-Step Method" — if it doesn't have a real name people actually use, just describe the idea.

### No self-narration
Don't announce or comment on your own points. Delete "this highlights," "this underscores," "this speaks to," "here's why this matters," "the key takeaway is," "now for the interesting part."

If the point landed, the narration is dead weight. If it didn't, narration won't save it.

## Phrases to Never Use

### Rhetorical transition questions
"The catch?" "The kicker?" "The brutal truth?" "But here's the thing." "So what does this mean?"

Only ask questions you'd actually ask someone.

## Words to Avoid

### Transition words
Arguably, Certainly, Consequently, Hence, However (as a sentence opener), Indeed, Moreover, Nevertheless, Nonetheless, Thus, Undoubtedly, Accordingly, Additionally, Notably, Essentially, Fundamentally, Inherently.

### Overused adjectives
Crucial, Cutting-edge, Dynamic, Exciting, Game-changing, Groundbreaking, Holistic, Innovative, Invaluable, Meticulous, Nuanced, Paramount, Pivotal, Profound, Remarkable, Robust, Seamless, Significant, Transformative, Unprecedented, Vital.

### Overused adverbs
Drastically, Genuinely, Meticulously, Profoundly, Remarkably, Significantly, Strategically, Truly.

### Abstract nouns that sound like AI
Bandwidth (figurative), Bedrock, Cadence, Catalyst, Cornerstone, Ecosystem (figurative), Framework (when vague), Game-changer, Guardrails (figurative), Innovation, Interplay, Intersection (figurative), Landscape (figurative), Linchpin, North star (figurative), Optimization, Pain point, Paradigm shift, Synergy, Takeaway, Transformation.

### Verbs to avoid
Aligns, Amplify, Augment, Bolster, Catalyze, Craft (figurative), Cultivate, Curate, Delve, Demystify, Dive in, Double down, Elevate, Embark, Empower, Enhance, Facilitate, Foster, Garner, Harness, Leverage, Maximize, Navigate (figurative), Reimagine, Resonate, Revolutionize, Showcase, Spearhead, Streamline, Underscore, Unlock (figurative), Unpack (figurative), Utilize.

## Language Patterns to Remove

### Copula avoidance
Replace "serves as," "stands as," "functions as," "represents" with "is."

Replace "boasts," "features," "offers" with "has."

AI avoids the word "is" for some reason — just use it.

### Participial phrase padding
Remove phrases added to the end of sentences purely for fake depth:
- "highlighting the importance of"
- "underscoring the need for"
- "reflecting a broader trend toward"
- "paving the way for"
- "shedding light on"

These add words without meaning.

### Synonym cycling
Don't call the same subject by different names in consecutive sentences. Pick one and stick with it.

### Filler
| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| In order to | To |
| Due to the fact that | Because |
| Has the ability to | Can |
| In the event that | If |
| It is important to note that | (delete entirely) |
| It's worth considering that | (delete entirely) |

### Vague attribution
Replace "experts say," "industry observers note," "some critics argue" with named sources, or cut the claim entirely.
